# LogCal AI Blog Image Prompts

Use these prompts in ChatGPT image generation for the new LogCal AI blog covers. Keep the images region-agnostic, useful, clean, and realistic. Avoid visible calorie numbers, medical claims, brand names other than LogCal AI, or tiny unreadable UI text.

## 1. How to Estimate Portion Sizes Without a Food Scale

Placement: blog cover image  
Size: 1200x760 landscape  
Alt text: `Visual portion size guide showing plate, bowl, cup, spoon, and handful examples`

Prompt:

```text
Create a premium editorial blog cover image for LogCal AI, an AI calorie tracker. Theme: portion size estimation. The image should immediately communicate "this article is about portion sizes" before someone reads the headline.

Scene: a bright, clean top-down layout focused on portion-size references. Show clear, labeled portion examples arranged like a simple visual guide. Include as many of these as can fit without overcrowding:
- quarter plate
- full plate
- cup
- bowl
- spoon
- hand or handful

Use small amounts of simple, generic food inside the references so they do not look empty or abstract. The user should instantly understand this is about estimating portion sizes, not meal inspiration. The image should feel like a friendly portion size guide, not a generic food photo.

Style: realistic lifestyle photography with subtle clean graphic accents, warm natural light, soft shadows, off-white background, green accents matching a friendly health app. Modern, calm, useful, and instructional without looking clinical.

Composition: landscape 1200x760. Use a neat grid or semi-grid arrangement so the portion references are obvious. Prioritize clarity over including every item. If six references feel crowded, include the best five. Keep enough breathing room between objects. No people, except the optional hand/handful reference.

Text: include minimal, clean labels if they help clarity, such as "Quarter plate", "Full plate", "Cup", "Bowl", "Spoon", "Hand". Labels should be large enough to read and spelled correctly. If labels look messy, use fewer labels rather than tiny text.

Avoid: making a full meal the main focus, weighing scales, diet culture imagery, before/after bodies, measuring tape around bodies, exaggerated weight-loss visuals, Indian-specific foods, clutter, stock-photo cheesiness, fake nutrition numbers, tiny unreadable labels.
```

## 2. Best Way to Track Calories for Weight Loss

Placement: blog cover image  
Size: 1200x760 landscape  
Alt text: `Consistent calorie tracking routine shown with daily meal logs and progress check marks`

Prompt:

```text
Create a premium editorial blog cover image for LogCal AI, an AI calorie tracker. Theme: consistency in calorie tracking for weight loss. The image should feel different from a portion-size or food-estimation image.

Scene: a clean, realistic tabletop showing a smartphone with a simple calorie tracking habit view: several days checked off, daily meal logs, and a gentle progress trend. Include small supporting elements like a notebook, calendar card, or repeated meal log cards to show consistency over time. Food should be minimal and secondary, not the main focus.

Style: realistic lifestyle photography with light app UI overlay elements, warm natural daylight, off-white tabletop, fresh green accents, modern health-tech feel. Friendly, practical, steady, and motivating without being intense.

Composition: landscape 1200x760. Make the phone, check marks, and repeated daily logs the main visual story. Use a simple weekly rhythm: Mon, Tue, Wed, Thu, Fri or five small checked cards. Leave breathing room and avoid clutter.

Text: if text appears, keep it minimal and generic: "Today", "Logged", "Meal 1", "Meal 2", or short weekday labels. No calorie numbers, no weight-loss promises, no fake statistics.

Avoid: making a plate of food the main subject, portion-size diagrams, dramatic weight-loss imagery, body transformation photos, scales, measuring tape, fake statistics, medical claims, clutter, overly glossy stock style, culturally specific foods.
```

## 3. Photo Calorie Tracking

Placement: blog cover image  
Size: 1200x760 landscape  
Alt text: `Phone camera scanning a meal plate for photo calorie tracking`

Prompt:

```text
Create a premium editorial blog cover image for LogCal AI, an AI calorie tracker. Theme: photo calorie tracking.

Scene: a smartphone camera view angled over a complete meal plate, as if the user is about to scan or log the meal. Add subtle visual cues like a soft focus frame, small check mark, or gentle scan line, but keep it realistic and not sci-fi.

Meal: region-agnostic balanced plate with vegetables, protein, carbs, and a small sauce or dressing on the side. Make the portions visible from above.

Style: realistic lifestyle photography, warm daylight, clean off-white background, green app accents, polished but natural. Useful, simple, friendly.

Composition: landscape 1200x760. The phone and plate should both be visible, with the plate clearly framed. Leave clean negative space for cropping.

Text: no text in the image except optional tiny generic UI labels like "Photo log" if needed.

Avoid: futuristic holograms, fake exact calorie numbers, messy food, overly dark lighting, medical imagery, diet culture symbols, Indian-specific foods, cluttered UI.
```

## 4. How to Build a Calorie Tracking Habit That Sticks

Placement: blog cover image  
Size: 1200x760 landscape  
Alt text: `Calorie tracking habit calendar with meal log cards and a phone`

Prompt:

```text
Create a premium editorial blog cover image for LogCal AI, an AI calorie tracker. Theme: building a calorie tracking habit that sticks.

Scene: a clean desk or kitchen counter with a smartphone showing a simple meal logging screen, a small weekly habit calendar with check marks, and a few minimal meal cards or food icons nearby. The feeling should be calm consistency, not strict dieting.

Style: realistic lifestyle photography with subtle illustrated UI accents, warm natural light, off-white and soft green palette, modern friendly health app aesthetic.

Composition: landscape 1200x760. Phone and habit calendar should be the main visual elements. Keep the image uncluttered and leave some negative space for responsive cropping.

Text: avoid large readable text. Tiny generic labels like "Today" or "Logged" are okay if they look natural.

Avoid: body photos, before/after imagery, weighing scales, guilt-based diet visuals, fake streak claims, harsh red warning colors, medical claims, region-specific foods.
```
